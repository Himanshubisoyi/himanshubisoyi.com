How to Choose a Website Developer: A 10-Point Checklist

A good website brings enquiries for years; a poor one costs money twice. Before you hire a freelancer or an agency, use this checklist to compare them fairly and avoid the most common problems.

By Himanshu Bisoyi, freelance website developer · November 2026

The checklist

Ask every developer you are considering about these ten points.

  • 1. Live portfolio. Ask for links to real websites, not only screenshots, and open them on your phone.
  • 2. Relevant experience. Have they built websites for businesses like yours, such as e-commerce stores, service companies or non-profits?
  • 3. A written, itemised quotation. Pages, features, platform, timeline and price should be listed clearly.
  • 4. Ownership. The domain, hosting and website must be registered to you, with full admin access handed over.
  • 5. Mobile and speed. Most visitors use phones. Ask how they make pages load fast.
  • 6. Basic SEO included. Page titles, descriptions, clean URLs and Google Search Console setup should be part of the work.
  • 7. Clear payment terms. A fair split is 50% in advance and 50% before launch.
  • 8. Support after launch. Ask what is covered for free, for how long, and what maintenance costs afterwards.
  • 9. Communication. You should know who you will talk to, how often you will get updates and how quickly they reply.
  • 10. Reviews. Read reviews on Google, Clutch, GoodFirms or LinkedIn, or ask to speak to a past client.

Warning signs

  • A price that is far below the market with no clear list of what is included.
  • The domain registered in the developer’s name instead of yours.
  • No contract, no timeline and no examples of live work.
  • Pressure to pay the full amount before any work is shown.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which platform do you recommend for my business, and why?
  • How many rounds of changes are included?
  • Who writes the content and provides the images?
  • How long will the project take, and what do you need from me?
  • What happens if something breaks after launch?

Frequently asked questions

Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

A freelancer is usually the better fit for small and medium business websites: you work directly with the developer and pay less. An agency suits large projects that need a team of designers, developers and project managers.

What should be included in a website quotation?

A clear list of pages and features, the platform, timeline, number of revision rounds, payment terms, what happens after launch (maintenance and support) and confirmation that the domain, hosting and website belong to you.

How much advance should I pay?

A common and fair arrangement is 50% in advance and 50% before the website goes live. Be cautious about paying the full amount upfront.

How do I check a developer’s past work?

Open the live websites in their portfolio on your phone, check how fast they load, and if possible speak to one or two past clients.
